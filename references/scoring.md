# Scoring

Use this reference when you want a more consistent, decision-oriented verdict on an internet tool, repo, or workflow.

## Score dimensions

Score each item from 1-5.

### 1. Practical utility
- 1 = mostly novelty or weak convenience
- 3 = useful in some recurring workflows
- 5 = clearly removes a repeated pain point

### 2. Local fit
- 1 = poorly matched to current OpenClaw environment
- 3 = usable with some adaptation
- 5 = works naturally with current tools/auth/setup

### 3. Operational burden
- 1 = heavy setup, many dependencies, annoying maintenance
- 3 = moderate setup with manageable friction
- 5 = low-friction, easy to keep working

### 4. Reliability
- 1 = highly fragile or frequently breaks
- 3 = mixed reliability / conditional success
- 5 = solid and predictable for normal use

### 5. Adoption worthiness
- 1 = should not adopt
- 3 = learn from it or adopt partially
- 5 = strong candidate for direct adoption

## Reading the result

- **21-25** → strong fit; direct adoption is plausible
- **16-20** → useful, but adopt selectively
- **10-15** → learn from it more than depend on it
- **5-9** → avoid unless there is a very specific need

## Reporting pattern

After scoring, summarize:
- total score
- strongest dimension
- weakest dimension
- final recommendation

## Rule

Do not let the numeric score replace judgment. If one severe risk dominates the outcome, say so clearly even if the total score looks decent.
